01 · Logo

The bluebird comes back, and it carries the old wave with it.

The brand started as Blubird Naturals, and the men's line is still Blakbird, but the current mark is a turquoise brush swirl with no bird in it. The rebuild puts a single perched bluebird at the center. Blue, it is Blu Naturals. Near-black, it is Blakbird. The two curved lines on the wing are the white stripes from the old swirl, so the shelf customer who knows the turquoise label still sees something familiar.

How it is built

Wordmark is Fraunces (soft axis) in lowercase, so it reads as handmade rather than clinical. The descriptor is Manrope, tracked wide. Everything is outlined paths, so the SVGs need no fonts installed. Minimum size: 24 px tall for the mark, 110 px wide for the horizontal lockup.

Clear space and use

Keep a margin equal to the bird's height around any lockup. On photos, use the reversed version over the dark end of the image or the one-colour version on a mist panel. Never put the bird on the old turquoise swirl, stretch it, or add a drop shadow.

Blakbird stays in the family

Same bird, same wordmark construction, ink instead of blue. On the current labels the men's line uses a hand-drawn crow that shares nothing with the parent brand. Now one shelf, one family.

Soft serif for the voice, clean sans for the shop.

Fraunces, soft axis

Fraunces carries the headlines, product names and pull quotes. Its soft variant has rounded terminals that feel like a hand-cut soap edge rather than a magazine. Manrope does everything else: body copy, prices, buttons, ingredient lists. Both are free on Google Fonts and load with one link tag in the WordPress theme, so no licensing and no self-hosting.

Set numerals in tabular figures wherever prices or weights line up. Uppercase labels get 0.1em of tracking. Body copy stays between 60 and 70 characters wide.

04 · Product imagery

Every product, cut from the wooden table and set on water.

All 41 product photos were cut out, white-balanced (the koa table was warming every white tube), and composed onto a paper-to-mist shoreline with a contact shadow, faint wave lines and light-through-water shapes. The outer rim of each image fades to the exact page color, so a card has no visible edge; the product appears to sit on the page itself. The treatment is a script, so new photos get the same look in seconds, in whichever colorway is chosen.

05 · The family

The thing Native and Lume cannot copy is the people making it.

Every competitor has coconut oil and arrowroot. None of them has a family in Honolulu infusing witch hazel with North Shore vanilla beans for three weeks. The site should make that impossible to miss, without turning into a diary.

Positioning line

Small-batch natural care, made by a family in Honolulu.

Supporting lines: "Ingredients you already know." · "Not an antiperspirant, on purpose." · "Made this week, not this year."

Voice

Warm, plain, specific. Say "coconut oil and clay" rather than "clean formulation". Explain the trade-offs out loud (it softens in heat; natural deodorant needs a two-week adjustment). Avoid "toxin-free" and "chemical-free": they invite the wrong argument and the FTC has started fining for them. Hawaiian words only where the family already uses them (ohana, mahalo): the discount codes show they do.

Where the family appears

  • Homepage: one photo block under the hero, three sentences, signed with first names, linking to the story page.
  • Product pages: a "who made this" strip with the maker's photo and the batch week.
  • Order confirmation and shipping emails: a photo and a one-line note.
  • Instagram: the making process is the content, see Social below.

Needed from the client: first names and roles of everyone in the family who makes or ships product, 10 to 20 candid photos of them at work (phone photos are fine, we will apply the same treatment), and the two-paragraph origin story in their own words. The story page and the product-page strips are templated and ready to drop these in.

06 · Blog from the Instagram archive

Old posts become the raw material. Pillar articles do the ranking.

Instagram captions republished as blog posts will not rank: they are short, undated in Google's eyes, and answer no search. The archive is still the best asset the brand has, because it holds four years of photos, customer comments and the founder's actual explanations. The plan uses it as sourcing for ten articles written around questions people type into Google, each embedding the original posts.

Process

  1. Client downloads the Instagram archive (Accounts Center → Your information and permissions → Download your information → JSON, all time). I parse it into a sheet: every post, caption, date, media file, tagged by theme.
  2. Posts are clustered under the ten pillars below. Each pillar gets 900 to 1,500 words written from the founder's captions and comments, with 3 to 6 archive photos embedded (treated like the product shots) and the original dates kept as "from the journal, June 2023".
  3. Each article links to two products and one other article; each product page links back to its article. Rank Math handles titles, meta, article schema and the sitemap. Product pages get product schema with price, weight and reviews.
  4. Publish two a week for five weeks, each with an Instagram post that links back, so the archive loop closes. After that, one new article a month from whatever the family posted that month.

07 · Email

Deodorant runs out in about sixty days. The email system is built around that.

The list, honestly

1,658contacts in Shopify457have bought at least once155bought twice or more103opted in to marketing~1,200accounts or abandoned checkouts with no order

Only the 103 opt-ins and the 457 buyers should be imported. Buyers are an existing customer relationship under CAN-SPAM; the launch email to them must carry a clear unsubscribe and an explicit "keep hearing from us" button so the list is clean from day one. The 1,200 non-buyers stay out.

Tool

Klaviyo, connected to WooCommerce. Free up to 250 active profiles, so the first months cost nothing, and its flows can branch on which product was bought, which is what the replenishment flow needs. MailPoet runs inside WordPress and is cheaper later, but its automations cannot do product-level timing without workarounds. Revisit only if the list passes 1,000 and the flows are not paying for the plan.

08 · Social

Three pillars, one creator program, and nothing that needs a studio.

Making it 3 reels / week

The pour, the stamp, the label, the packing table. Thirty seconds, phone on a stand, no talking required. This is the proof behind "small batch" and the only content competitors cannot produce.

Explaining it 2 carousels / week

One ingredient, one trade-off, one myth. The ten blog pillars give forty carousels without new research. Each carousel's last slide links to the article.

Customers and creators daily stories

Reposts of tagged photos, review screenshots, creator clips. Ask for the tag on the day-20 email and on the box insert.

Creator program. One code, rewild, produced 125 orders, 13% of everything the store has ever sold. That is a channel, not luck. Formalize it: ten micro-creators (nurses, hikers, runners, mothers of young kids, the audiences already buying), each with a 10% code and 10% commission tracked in WooCommerce with a free affiliate plugin, a starter kit of three deodorants and a soap, and one brief: show the two-week switch. Recruit the first five from existing customers who post; the orders export shows who they are.

TikTok Shop and Pinterest are second-year channels. Pinterest first: the soap and flat-lay imagery is Pinterest-shaped, it is search-driven like the blog, and the pins can carry the same product cards.

10 · Decisions and next steps

Five decisions unlock the build.

Decide

  • Colorway. Tidepool recommended; the switcher above shows all three on the real storefront pieces.
  • Prices. $12 sits under Native ($13 to $14) and Lume ($15 to $17). With this identity $14 is defensible, and the 3-pack and subscription discounts need a base to come off. Also settles the tax-inclusive question already flagged in the Shopify settings notes.
  • Free-shipping threshold. $40 proposed; depends on margin per stick.
  • Labels. Every product photo still shows the old swirl. Launch can go ahead with the old labels, but the label redesign should follow immediately, before a second photo shoot.
  • Email tool. Klaviyo proposed; needs the client's sign-off because it holds the customer list.

Send

  • Instagram archive export (JSON, all time).
  • Family names, roles, 10 to 20 photos at work, the origin story in their words.
  • The three lifestyle shots listed under Imagery.
  • Navigation menu contents, policies, and the two page HTMLs still missing from the Shopify capture.

Then: lock the chosen colorway into a design-system reference the theme build reads from, write the ten articles from the archive, design the new labels on the same system, and build the WooCommerce theme against the homepage and product-page layouts shown here.
