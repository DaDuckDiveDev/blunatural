Most people who tell us natural deodorant "doesn't work" gave up on day four. That is the worst day. Here is what is going on, and what to do about it.

Why it gets worse before it gets better

Antiperspirant blocks sweat with aluminum salts. When you stop, the ducts reopen and for a week or two you sweat more than you will later, while the bacteria that cause odor are still the antiperspirant-era population. A deodorant handles odor; it does not plug anything. So in week one you have more sweat and the old bacteria. By week two the microbiome shifts and the sweat settles.

Day by day

  • Days 1–3: feels fine, maybe slightly damp. Apply two or three swipes, not six.
  • Days 4–7: the dip. More smell than usual by afternoon. Reapply once; wash with plain soap at night.
  • Days 8–14: settles. Most people stop noticing their underarms at all.
  • After: one application in the morning holds.

Three things that help

  • Less product. A thin layer absorbs; a thick one sits on top and stains shirts.
  • A bar of soap at night (ours or any plain one) instead of body wash with fragrance.
  • If you itch, stop. That is baking soda, not the adjustment. Switch to the baking-soda-free stick.

When it is not the adjustment

Itching or a red patch within the first days is a baking soda reaction, and waiting will not fix it. Swap to the Baking Soda Free stick; same scents, bentonite clay instead. If essential oils are the issue, the Essential Oil Free or Unscented stick.
