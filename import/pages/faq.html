Does natural deodorant actually work?

Yes, for odor. It does not stop sweat, because it is not an antiperspirant and contains no aluminum. Baking soda or bentonite clay neutralize the bacteria that cause smell; arrowroot and kaolin clay absorb moisture so you feel dry. Most people are fully switched over in about two weeks.

What happens in the first two weeks?

If you are coming off an antiperspirant, your underarms may sweat and smell more than usual for a week or two while the pores clear. Apply a little less than you think you need, reapply once in the afternoon if you want, and give it the full two weeks before judging it. The switching guide has the day-by-day version.

My stick arrived soft. Is it ruined?

No. It is mostly coconut oil, which softens above about 76°F, and a mailbox in July is hotter than that. Stand it upright in the fridge for an hour and it sets back up. Nothing in it spoils. Store it at room temperature after that, out of direct sun.

I got a rash from a natural deodorant before.

Almost always that is baking soda. Start with the Baking Soda Free stick, or the Unscented if you also want to avoid essential oils. The four-question quiz sorts this out in thirty seconds.

How long does a stick last?

About two months with daily use. Two or three swipes is enough; more does not work better, it just takes longer to absorb.
